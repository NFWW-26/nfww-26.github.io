About New Forest Water Watch

Who We Are

New Forest Water Watch (NFWW) is an independent, community-led group working to improve water quality, protect biodiversity, and strengthen environmental protection across the New Forest. We unite residents, volunteers, scientists, public bodies, councils, river users, and other organisations who care about the health of our rivers, streams, and the wildlife and people that depend on them.

We believe environmental decisions should be based on sound scientific evidence and that local communities play a vital role in understanding and protecting our natural environment.

With 120 volunteers monitoring over 80 sites every month, NFWW has developed one of the largest community water-monitoring networks in the region. Our work spans from the River Avon in the west to the Waterside in the east, and from the River Test in the north to the Solent in the south.

How We Monitor Our Rivers and Streams

A Community-Led Scientific Monitoring Programme

NFWW runs a coordinated programme of regular water-quality monitoring. Our trained volunteers collect data monthly at over 80 sites, building a detailed picture of river and stream health.

We measure chemical, microbiological, and environmental indicators that:

  • Provide evidence of pollution
  • Identify possible causes and impacts
  • Track changes over time

1. Routine Water-Quality Testing

Volunteers routinely test for three key indicators at every site:

  • Nitrates: Naturally occurring, but high levels may indicate pollution from agriculture, sewage, or fertilisers, contributing to excessive plant and algal growth.
  • Phosphates: Essential for plant growth, but excessive amounts can lead to eutrophication, mainly from sewage and agricultural run-off. Phosphate levels are generally low in the New Forest.
  • E. coli: Bacteria indicating faecal contamination from sewage, livestock, or wildlife. Some strains can cause illness. Our testing reports concentrations as colony-forming units (CFU) per 100 ml of water.

2. Additional Testing When Pollution Is Suspected

If routine tests suggest pollution, we do further tests, including:

  • Ammonia: Toxic to aquatic life; indicates recent sewage contamination.
  • Dissolved Oxygen: Essential for fish and aquatic organisms; low levels can stress or kill wildlife.
  • Electrical Conductivity: Measures salt and mineral content, helping detect unusual water chemistry changes.

3. Environmental Observations

Volunteers also record conditions to help interpret water-quality results, including:

  • Air and water temperature
  • Water colour and turbidity (cloudiness)
  • Riverbank vegetation
  • Site flow, weather, rainfall, and surroundings

4. Investigating Pollution Incidents

When pollution is suspected, we undertake targeted sampling, including upstream and downstream sites, to understand its source, spread, and impact. We collaborate with the Environment Agency to address incidents effectively.

5. Building a Picture Over Time

Every test is a snapshot influenced by weather, flow, season, and activities nearby. Regular monitoring creates a strong evidence base to identify patterns and trends that help protect New Forest’s freshwater environments.

Our results are shared via maps, reports, and an open-data platform accessible to everyone, from residents to researchers.

Our Volunteers

NFWW is supported by 120 volunteers from diverse backgrounds, all committed to protecting New Forest’s waterways. Volunteers receive training, equipment, and guidance to collect data safely and consistently.

Working in small teams, they visit monitoring sites monthly, conducting tests and recording observations. Some also support biodiversity monitoring, data analysis, and community outreach. Every volunteer and site contributes to the bigger picture.

Get Involved

There are always opportunities to join monitoring teams, though most new volunteers join existing site teams. No scientific background is required. Training and equipment are provided.
